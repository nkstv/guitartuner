Tuning guide

How to tune an acoustic guitar

Standard acoustic guitar tuning is E A D G B E, from the thickest string to the thinnest. A quick tuning check before you play keeps chords clean, open strings balanced and your whole instrument sounding more musical.

01 — Method

Tune it step by step

01

Start with the low E string

Open the free tuner, choose Acoustic guitar and begin with E2, the thickest string.

02

Play one string at a time

Pluck the string clearly and let it ring. Avoid touching the other strings while the tuner is listening.

03

Adjust the tuning peg slowly

If the pitch is flat, tighten the string a little. If it is sharp, loosen it slightly.

04

Tune up to the note

For better stability, approach the target pitch from below instead of overshooting it.

05

Repeat E A D G B E

Move through all six strings, then check them once again because changing one string can slightly affect the others.

02 — Tips

A few tips that make tuning easier

  • New acoustic strings stretch a lot during the first hours. Retune several times.
  • Use a medium, clean pluck. Hitting the string too hard can make the first pitch reading unstable.
  • If a chord still sounds wrong after tuning, check string condition and intonation.

03 — FAQ

Quick answers

How often should I tune an acoustic guitar?

Ideally before every playing session, and again whenever the instrument has been moved, exposed to temperature changes or fitted with new strings.

Which direction should I turn the tuning peg?

The safe rule is to watch the pitch rather than rely on a direction: turn very slightly, pluck again and confirm whether the note moved up or down.
